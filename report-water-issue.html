Common Water Problems and Quick Fixes

Not every issue means something's wrong with the district's system. A clogged or closed shut-off valve, a faulty pressure regulator, or mineral buildup in a faucet aerator causes many residential water problems — and you may be able to fix it yourself in a few minutes.

Low Water Pressure

The cause: Mineral deposits or debris trapped in the faucet aerator.

What to try: Unscrew the small screen (aerator) at the tip of the faucet, clean out any white crust or dirt, and screw it back on. Also check that your main water shut-off valve is fully open.

Sudden Loss of Water in One Fixture

The cause: A localized supply line kink, a closed supply valve under the sink, or a blocked cartridge.

What to try: Look under the sink or behind the toilet. Turn the small oval or round handle clockwise and counter-clockwise to make sure the valve is fully open.

Suspected Hidden Leaks or High Bills

The cause: A running toilet, a dripping faucet, or an underground service line leak.

What to try: Perform a water meter test — turn off all indoor and outdoor water use, then check the small red or black triangle/dial on your water meter. If it moves, there's a leak somewhere inside your property line. Check your toilet flappers first.

Discolored or Smelly Water

The cause: Local main flushing, water heater sediment, or corroding household pipes.

What to try: Run the cold water tap outside or in the bathtub for a few minutes to see if it clears. If only the hot water smells or looks off, flush your water heater tank.
