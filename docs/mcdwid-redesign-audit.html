Part II

Redesign Audit

A side-by-side review of the live site against the in-progress redesign, prepared for the board / planning meeting, plus a proposed information architecture and next steps.

01

Executive summary

The live site is content-heavy and operationally real — board rosters, real fee schedules, active water-quality advisories, downloadable forms — but visually dated and spread across a flat, deep navigation menu. The redesign has a stronger visual foundation (a real design system, a chosen color direction, logo concepts in progress) but still holds a fraction of the live site's real content: 11 pages exist against roughly 17 distinct pages/sections on the live site, up from 6 as of v6 — see "What changed since v6" above.

The gap is no longer primarily structural — it's that the real content itself (staff names, board roster, district history, itemized rates, active compliance notices, service-application requirements) still hasn't been brought into the redesign's now-built pages. Two pages, service-area.html and pay-bill.html, are built but still only reachable from in-content links on the homepage, not the primary nav.

Highest-priority item The live water-quality page carries active health-relevant advisories (arsenic and nitrate exceedances, boil-water-style warnings for specific neighborhoods). This content changes over time and can't be treated as placeholder copy — it needs a defined process for who supplies and approves it before the redesign launches. See §6.

Recommendation: settle the site's remaining information architecture questions (§11) in this meeting — two of them (About/Governance and Services structure) were already acted on between v6 and v7; confirm those calls were right before content is written against them, since it's far cheaper to change now than after.

Governance & about content

The live site carries real institutional history the redesign doesn't have yet:

  • District formed July 21, 1986 by the Pinal County Board of Supervisors, to acquire the area's sole private water utility.
  • Designated a Colonia in February 1998 (federal underserved-community designation, often tied to infrastructure funding eligibility).
  • November 2017: merged with New Saddleback Vista DWID — this merger is where "Consolidated" enters the name.
  • November 2020: Valle Escondido DWID merged in after a district election.
  • Board: 4 members, all current terms expiring 2026 — Lucia Rodriguez (Chairman), Debbie Chitwood (Vice Chairman), Julia Gusse, Odilia Saldana. Meets 2nd Tuesday, 4:00 PM, at the district office.
  • Staff: 2 named people — Adam Hazen (Field Manager), Justin Quinn (Office Manager). Emergency operations and admin are handled through intergovernmental agreements with 4 other districts, not a large in-house team.
Decision recorded — 2026-08-18 Staff and Board were combined into a single "About & Governance" page (about.html) rather than two dedicated pages, matching what §11's proposed IA already assumed. This is a structural decision only — none of the history, board roster, or staff-directory content above has been migrated into the built page yet. Flag at the next meeting if separate pages are actually preferred; it's a cheap reversal at this stage.

06

Water quality & compliance

Compliance risk The live site currently lists active drinking-water advisories: an arsenic/nitrate exceedance notice, a Valle Escondido water disclosure (Nov 2025), and a Saddleback Vista drinking-water warning (2025). Water quality advisories are regulated communications — the redesign cannot launch with placeholder copy in this section, and whoever owns compliance at the district needs to be the one supplying current text, not this redesign process.

Reports on file by community, per the live site: New Saddleback Vista (2025, 2024), Maricopa Consolidated (2025, 2024, 2022 — 2023 appears absent from their own archive), Valle Escondido (2025, 2024, 2021, 2019). The live site also gives practical nitrate guidance (reverse-osmosis filtration, $75–$500 typical cost) and notes only 3 years of reports are kept on hand at the office.

Discuss as a group Should the new site organize CCR reports by community (mirroring the three merged districts) the way the live site does, or consolidate under one "Maricopa Consolidated" identity now that the merger is several years settled?
07

News, notices & meeting archive

The live site's notice list mixes genuinely current items with stale ones — a "new office location" announcement from October 2019 is still listed as news, alongside a 2023 fraud-sampler warning and a 2021 "current projects" notice. There's no visible archiving or date-based cutoff.

The board meeting archive is organized by year (2026, 2025, 2024, 2023, 2021 — 2022 is missing) and mixes file formats inconsistently: most agendas/minutes are PDF, but at least one set of February 2026 minutes is a TIFF image — not text-searchable, not accessible to screen readers.

Recommendation Two easy wins the redesign can offer over the live site: (1) a real "archived" state for notices past a certain age instead of leaving them in the active feed indefinitely, and (2) a house rule that meeting minutes/agendas are PDF only, never scanned image formats like TIFF.
08

Design & branding status

  • Live site tagline: "Committed to Providing Clean, Safe Water for All Our Residents." Redesign homepage copy: "Water service made simpler for our community." These read as two different positioning ideas — worth an explicit decision rather than letting it default.
  • Color scheme: the redesign's master branch currently uses the "Corporate Blue" palette (steel blue on deep navy), chosen from five alternatives built during this process. Four other palettes exist on branches if the direction should be revisited.
  • Logo: still undecided. Multiple concepts exist (a water-droplet mark, a desert-horizon mark, an MC monogram, a canal-M mark, a Sonoran-flow mark, a modern-wave-M mark, and three wordmark-integrated concepts with a spout/valve/land-water motif built into the letters). None chosen yet — worth a decision before the WordPress build, since the header/favicon depend on it.
  • Visual maturity: the live site is functional but visually dated (2019-era municipal-CMS look, per Streamline). The redesign is a clear visual upgrade as far as it's built — the gap is content coverage, not design quality.

Unchanged since v6 — no logo, palette, or tagline decisions were made in the 2026-08-18 build session (see changelog).
